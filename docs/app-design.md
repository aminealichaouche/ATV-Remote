# ATV Remote: implemented design

ATV Remote has a native SwiftUI implementation targeting iOS 27.0 or later. The iPhone connects directly to Samsung TVs and Apple TVs on the same home network. Runtime use needs no relay, cloud service, or always-on computer. The source implementation and its simulator checks do not establish that a particular physical TV works; hardware pairing and command behavior remain to be tested.

## Device and room selection

The main screen starts with an explicit room Menu at the top left and an adjacent settings gear. Its selected-name Text stays on one line, scales down by up to 20%, then truncates long names; VoiceOver keeps the full room name. A fixed chevron and 44-point gear/Shortcuts controls avoid native Picker label wrapping. When all controls fit, it uses a non-scrolling layout; otherwise they remain vertically scrollable. Directly beneath the room menu, a horizontal connection band places Apple TV on the left and Samsung on the right. The equal-width cells contain a one-line caption name, then its 20-point type icon and eight-point green/gray dot, with six points of spacing. A disconnected name/icon/dot is a single 44-point tap target for retry, PIN entry or certificate review, without a separate action row. Pending connections show a spinner beside the dot and accept taps to cancel/restart; connected indicators remain informational. A single selected TV uses the available width; no selection hides the band. Long names truncate in the middle. VoiceOver keeps the full type/name/state announcement and ignores decorative icons/dots. Gray helper text and duplicate device captions are removed. Settings uses the current room name for both its top title and the TV section heading. Independent Apple TV and Samsung TV menus use identical full-width stacked labels: TV type above the selected name and address, with matching trailing chevrons. Each connection line has its status on the left and Disconnect or Connect on the right. Manage TVs, Manage rooms, and Transfer setup push onto that same navigation stack, with native top-left back buttons. Saved TVs have individual cards with a padded name/address header, a separate Manage TV row and a separate 44-point action row. Done closes Settings. Device menus include a friendly name and address so similarly named TVs can be distinguished. A separate Apple TV / Samsung segmented control chooses where navigation, Back, Home, and Play/Pause go. Volume down, volume up, and mute always target the selected Samsung, including while navigating Apple TV.

Each saved room contains optional Samsung and Apple TV device identities. Either may be None. Choosing a room selects its saved pair; a Samsung-only room selects Samsung navigation. Individual dropdown changes preserve the room's saved defaults and expose **Save this pair for [room]** when the selection differs. The Rooms screen creates, edits, and deletes room groups. Device names can be changed without re-pairing.

Adding a TV saves its profile and selects it temporarily for pairing. Existing complete and partial room groups remain unchanged. A transient initial setup starts only for a fully empty room and a genuinely new TV, and can assign one new Samsung and one new Apple TV without replacing a slot. Explicit saves, actual room/selection changes, duplicates, additional same-kind devices, removal, re-pair/cancel, import, and background end this initial setup. Reselecting an unchanged item preserves it. Later assignments require Save this pair or a room edit. Removing a TV clears its saved room references and deletes that TV's pairing from the iPhone. Re-pair deletes that device's saved authentication before explicitly starting fresh setup even if it is already selected.

Established sessions are retained by stable TV identity for visited rooms. Selecting another TV preserves the old actor, socket, state and observation token when connected; selecting it again restores that actor without restarting its connection. Incomplete connection/pairing work is cancelled and its actor closed. Commands and tools remain selected-target/epoch scoped, so controls cannot transfer to another room. Only selected offline TVs automatically reconnect; unselected established sessions continue receiving state callbacks and foreground transport checks. Manual Disconnect holds still belong to TV identities. Auto trials remain bounded to four and only their adopted winner enters the retained-session collection.

## Controls in this build

| Control | Destination and behavior |
|---|---|
| D-pad, OK, Back, Play/Pause | Selected navigation target; single button presses |
| Home | Single tap: selected Apple TV sends one-second Back/Menu hold to reach its app grid, independent of its TV-button setting; Samsung sends its usual Home key. Apple TV only: double tap sends TV-button double-tap for the app switcher, hold sends TV-button hold for Control Center. |
| Touchpad | Discrete swipes become directional presses; tap selects; a gesture is canceled if the target changes |
| TV volume and mute | Selected Samsung; mute toggles and does not claim an observed mute state |
| Keyboard | Selected Apple TV; checks for an active text field and appends typed text, up to 16 KB per send |
| Apps | Registers an optional TVRC remote session, requests the selected Apple TV’s installed apps, and launches a returned app identifier; TV errors remain visible with a reload button |
| Inputs | Opens the selected Samsung's source menu; no verified direct HDMI shortcut |
| Power | Wake/sleep the selected connected Apple TV; request Samsung power-on using its saved MAC even when disconnected; send one power toggle to a connected Samsung |

The app reports **Sent to [device]** after a command send succeeds. In particular, Samsung transport completion does not acknowledge that the TV applied the key; the visible response on the television must be checked. Controls require a connected session. Commands are not saved for replay after reconnect. Rapid taps while a main-screen command is in progress may be ignored; held-key repeat and continuous Apple TV touch events are not implemented.

Samsung **Turn on** uses a separate directed Wake-on-LAN request. It requires that TV's active-interface MAC, an on-link IPv4 address, and enabled standby networking; the remote connection can be offline. Set it up once under Power or Manage TVs. UDP submission shows a request-sent message and does not mark the TV connected; use Connect after startup. Power **Toggle** still sends one `KEY_POWER` click and must not be automatically retried. A discrete off-only command remains unsupported. Apple TV wake requires an available Companion connection; if it cannot be discovered or connected while asleep, wake it physically first. Visible behavior varies with firmware.

The separate conversation preview remains simulated, including its example rooms and devices. The native app begins with an empty device list and a Living Room profile.

## Remote layouts

Settings has a **Remote layout** selector with **Expanded Apple TV remote** first, **Apple TV remote**, and **Alternate** (formerly Standard). Expanded is the default for both remote views and shared preferences when no layout is saved. Existing stored raw values are unchanged, so explicit choices survive updates. The alternative follows the pictured Siri Remote: Back/Home row, Play/Pause and Mute in a left column, and a single vertical +/− rocker in the right column. Power is a 44-point button above the pad at the right. The alternative uses a 200-point pad, 64-point circular controls and a 64×144-point rocker. Alternate keeps its existing 228-point pad and horizontal action/volume rows. Both have a 24-point pad-to-buttons gap and preserve Samsung volume routing. ViewThatFits still avoids scrolling whenever the controls fit.

Expanded keeps the same Siri Remote button arrangement and Power placement, with the Alternate pad dimensions: a 228-point circular D-pad or a full-content-width touchpad 228 points high with 28-point corners. D-pad control sizes and internal gaps match Alternate. The screen retains 18-point side margins. Circular controls grow from 64 to 76 points; horizontal spacing grows from 20 to 36 points and vertical spacing from 16 to 24 points. The continuous rocker grows to 76×176 points, and the pad-to-buttons gap grows to 32 points. Larger content scrolls only when it does not fit the viewport.

The alternative enum case retains the raw value `stackedVolume`, so saved Stacked preferences migrate without resetting. `@AppStorage` uses the existing `RoomRemote.remoteLayout.v1` layout key and the new Bool `RoomRemote.useTouchpad.v1` pad key. Both settings are per iPhone, survive relaunch, and apply across rooms; neither is included in encrypted sharing. Changing pad mode cancels a captured gesture before swapping the surface.

Power presents separate compact On/Off controls for each device. Samsung’s header gear opens power-on setup; On also opens it when the MAC is absent. Wake ownership/cancellation and one-shot power commands remain in the store. Samsung Off uses the existing single power toggle while connected; there is no verified discrete-off acknowledgement. The removed helper paragraph’s technical limits are recorded in the [power audit](atv-remote-power-simplification-audit.md).

## Live camera setup

Samsung IP and MAC text fields have trailing 44-point camera text buttons. Both open a VisionKit `DataScannerViewController` configured for English text only. Recognition runs on the iPhone while pointing at the TV’s Network Status or About This TV text. It stages up to eight private IPv4 and eight Wi-Fi/Ethernet MAC candidates across menus. QR codes and still photos are not inputs. Raw camera images and transcripts are never saved or uploaded.

Review removes the camera controller. The user checks the addresses, chooses among ambiguous candidates, and can edit unclear characters. Applying fills the unsaved setup fields; adding/pairing or saving a wake MAC still requires its separate button. Manual names and absent fields remain unchanged. Existing-TV power-on setup names the target, shows one labelled read-only IP row, asks only for its MAC, and blocks any conflicting scanned IP; it never rewrites that device’s IP, pairing or room assignments.

Camera permission, device support and availability are checked before creating the scanner. Denial or unavailable hardware leaves manual entry accessible. The sheet starts from existing setup drafts. Backgrounding clears detected candidates, restores the original drafts and invalidates callbacks. Parsing uses complete labeled values from a current frame, with bounded input and the app’s existing address validators. Gateway/DNS/subnet and known Bluetooth/remote-control labels are ignored. It does not repair OCR characters or guarantee that every model’s menu labels will be recognized. See [camera scan audit](atv-remote-live-text-audit.md).

## Apps and per-TV stars

Apps lists **Starred**, then **Downloaded**, then **Included**, omitting empty sections. A separate 44-point star button changes the selected Apple TV's preference without launching the app; tapping its name launches it. Rows use the selected TV's current returned names and exact bundle IDs, with alphabetical ordering and an ID tie-breaker. Starred apps move out of their original section so each app appears once.

Included membership uses the exact, case-sensitive identifiers from [Apple's tvOS app reference](https://support.apple.com/guide/deployment/bundle-ids-for-apple-tv-apple-apps-depcdd66fe58/web). Companion returns bundle IDs and names without an origin flag. Unrecognized apps remain in Downloaded, including unrecognized Apple identifiers. This is an inference from the published list; future native apps can require an allowlist update. [Classification audit](atv-remote-app-classification-audit.md).

The registry owns separate bundle-ID sets keyed by stable saved Apple TV identities. A missing/null favorites field migrates to empty when an older registry loads; room/device and Keychain identities remain unchanged. Mutations publish only after the atomic registry save succeeds. Names, room edits, re-pairing, and recovery preserve preferences; removing a TV clears only its stars. Preference storage is bounded to 256 IDs per TV and 1024 total, with identifiers no longer than 512 bytes.

**Import stars from another Apple TV** pushes a source-picker screen with native back navigation. After a successful destination app-list load, the user can choose any other saved Apple TV, review the saved and installed-match counts, and confirm. The copy unions source and destination stars without modifying the source or requiring its network connection. Imported identifiers absent from the destination list stay hidden and become starred if installed later. No source rows or phantom launch targets are created. This is a one-time copy; later changes remain independent. Full setup transfers include per-TV stars and layout/pad preferences. Apple TV controller identities are excluded; each iPhone pairs independently.

## App presentation

Apps displays each installed app’s name with a separate star button. There are no app thumbnails, fallback tiles or App Store info links. The former public catalog lookup, image download, cache implementation and its obsolete tests were removed in 0.3.17 at the user’s request. Earlier artwork reports describe historical builds only. The current app does not use or read the previous artwork cache; iOS manages any old files left in its purgeable Caches directory.

The Companion app list still supplies exact identifiers and names from the selected Apple TV. Installed-only sections, per-TV stars, imports, launch actions and error handling remain. No artwork lookup is made when opening Apps.

## Built-in shortcuts

The main row includes an always-visible top-right **Shortcuts** control with the official Apple Shortcuts app artwork, bundled as a local image asset. Its 44-point target opens **Restart Xfinity Stream** and **Restart current app**, compiled into every new build. This panel operates on the selected Apple TV. The image's [provenance record](../design/assets/apple-shortcuts-icon-provenance.json) identifies the exact Apple CDN asset; it is stored unchanged and scaled only when rendered.

Xfinity requires a live session, touch-swipe capability, and exact installed bundle ID `com.comcast.cim.x2`, verified from Apple's tvOS app metadata. One tap checks the installed list, launches Xfinity, waits for its animation, double-presses the TV button to open the switcher, waits, sends one full-height upward flick, exits with Back and launches Xfinity again. There is no card-confirmation step. The flick stays at x = 500 and moves y = 1000 to 0 through twelve paced 16 ms holds, including a held endpoint, then releases there after another 16 ms. The 208 ms wait budget excludes transport and validation time.

**Restart current app** opens a native installed-app chooser with a top-left back button. Companion does not reliably identify the foreground app, so the user identifies it; the same macro then launches, swipes and relaunches that exact ID. A fresh installed-list check and original TV/session validation precede effects. Other Apple TV command work is excluded during the run. Closing the sheet, backgrounding, selection/session changes and errors stop it; no dismissal or timed-out command is retried. Only a release can clean up an already started touch on its original sender.

Companion exposes neither a targeted force-quit command nor switcher-card identity. Automatic dismissal assumes the newly launched app's card is focused; a different controller or overlay could change that. The final label below both shortcut buttons reports **[app] restart sent**, without claiming verified termination. The gesture and its focus assumption need physical-TV testing. [Primary-source audit and limitations](atv-remote-xfinity-shortcut-audit.md).

The Apple command slot remains reserved through the user check and completion. Apple app/keyboard tools cannot interleave; Samsung control has a separate slot. Every remote step validates the original device, connection generation, workflow context, and cancellation before and after awaits. Dismissal, selection changes, backgrounding, disconnects, and errors release ownership and stop future steps. Touch cancellation attempts one release on the original controller and retains its driver reservation until that local write finishes; it does not reconnect, retry a dismissal, or route to the next selected TV. No separate wall-clock deadline is added to the vendor's one-way event writer. Already delivered gestures cannot be undone. Pending workflows are transient and are never restored or shared with pairing/setup data.

## Pairing and recovery

Samsung setup accepts a friendly name and a **literal private IP address**, including supported IPv6 unique-local addresses. It rejects URLs, hostnames, public addresses, and unsupported ports. The adapter uses TLS 1.2 or later and the secure Tizen WebSocket on port 8002. There is no unencrypted port-8001 fallback or legacy Samsung protocol.

First connection displays the selected name, address, and optional SHA-256 certificate fingerprint. **Trust this TV and request access** stores that exact certificate fingerprint for this device, then reconnects. The user must approve the **Room Remote** request on the Samsung. Only the expected authorized connection event marks the session connected. Denial and approval timeout produce failures. A changed saved certificate is rejected; inspect the TV and address before deliberately using Re-pair to reset that identity. First-use trust depends on selecting the intended TV on a trusted home network.

Apple TV setup uses Bonjour discovery, then the four-digit PIN shown on the selected television. The driver uses Companion pairing and verifies the stored peer identity on reconnect. Discovery identity resolves address and port changes; an unavailable or ambiguous identity fails instead of connecting to a different Apple TV at the old address. An incorrect PIN or a canceled attempt does not establish a connected session. Cancel failed pairing and Connect again to request a fresh code.

The iOS Local Network permission prompt is triggered by actual network use. The Your TVs screen includes **Open iPhone app settings** for permission recovery. Connection states distinguish connecting, awaiting approval, awaiting PIN, certificate review, connected, and failure. Disconnect, Connect, Enter code, Review TV, and Cancel actions follow those states. Manual Disconnect leaves Settings open, cancels only the selected kind’s transport and work, invalidates its stale callbacks, and preserves pairing, selections, room assignments, stars, and wake metadata.

When leaving the foreground, the app retains established selected and unselected sessions, cancels commands/discovery and incomplete setup, and stops the foreground status monitor. Returning keeps existing green indicators while immediately verifying all established sessions concurrently. Apple TV uses a bounded `_systemInfo` response on its original authenticated channel, with the same client identity; Samsung uses a correlated WebSocket ping/pong. Neither check sends HID, launch, session setup or power commands. Cancellation does not close otherwise healthy sessions. Failure downgrades the affected status; selected TVs remain eligible for normal reconnection. Regular readiness polling continues every three seconds. Per-TV observation tokens reject old-actor callbacks and late check results after session replacement. iOS process termination closes sockets; cold launch restores pairings and connects automatically rather than persisting false green states.

## Architecture and storage

Samsung wake metadata is an optional `wakeMACAddress` on each saved Samsung profile. Old profiles decode with nil, and the version 1 transfer payload can carry the additional nonsecret field inside legacy or new encrypted envelopes. Recipient reuse preserves the existing profile's MAC; new imports validate owner kind and value. Manual entry uses the active Ethernet/Wi-Fi MAC; unreliable REST metadata is not fetched. Pairing refresh and room changes preserve stored wake metadata, IDs, pins, tokens, and stars.

`SamsungWakeSender` validates the target against active local IPv4 interface masks before opening a socket, rejects self/network/broadcast addresses, binds the chosen local IP, prohibits cellular and peer-to-peer, and sends one 102-byte UDP port-9 datagram. A five-second timeout and cancellation close the socket. Actual callback ownership uses a one-shot UUID gate; stale callbacks cannot send twice or clear a newer operation. The store holds the Samsung command slot and captured device/endpoint/MAC/epoch, canceling on selection, metadata edits, dismissal, or backgrounding. No remote keys are queued for after wake.

This release supports on-link subnets with valid /1–/30 masks and retains the existing private-IP parser's conservative rejection of addresses ending in .255. IPv6, routed/VPN-only paths, broadcast, and new entitlements are unused. Unicast delivery can depend on standby ARP behavior after cache expiry, so physical long-standby tests are needed. [Primary-source wake audit](atv-remote-samsung-wake-audit.md).

Apple TV uses the locally vendored SwiftATV source based on audited revision `2cc34befa6826f3824846a73d0a1f674ee7688fe`, with local security changes. Samsung uses Apple Network and Security APIs rather than either user-supplied Python project. The earlier reports document upstream snapshots and adoption decisions; they are not a claim that every dependency or the new app has undergone exhaustive verification. Third-party license notices are included in `App/Resources/ThirdPartyNotices.txt`.

Apple credentials, Samsung tokens, and certificate fingerprints live in a nonsynchronizing app-scoped Keychain service with `kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`. Names, addresses, room assignments, and the last selected room use an atomic local registry file with a preferences fallback for the 0.1 update. A nonsecret journal preserves unassigned profiles after an interrupted import. Room names do not serve as credential keys. The app does not intentionally log PINs, typed text, pairing tokens, or private keys, and includes no telemetry or cloud command endpoint.

The app declares `_companion-link._tcp`, `_device-info._tcp`, and `_sleep-proxy._udp` Bonjour service types and a Local Network usage description. It implements directed Samsung wake without multicast discovery or a custom multicast entitlement. Manual Samsung IP/MAC addressing remains the setup path.

## Four-word transfer phrases

New setup exports display four independently sampled random words from 7,772 ASCII entries derived from EFF's long wordlist. Unbiased UInt32 rejection sampling selects each word with replacement; repetitions and word order remain significant. Spaces, tabs, line breaks, hyphens and ASCII capitals normalize to four lowercase words. Inputs are capped at 128 bytes and must contain exactly four complete dictionary words. This dictionary is part of the version 2 format and must be preserved for future imports.

A version 2 encrypted envelope declares only the fixed supported `pbkdf2-sha256` KDF and 600,000 iterations. CommonCrypto derives a 32-byte key from the phrase with a random 16-byte salt and a version/KDF-specific domain. AES-GCM authenticates that same domain and salt. Unsupported metadata, downgrade attempts and malformed bounds reject before expensive work. Legacy version 1 files retain their exact original code/HKDF/AAD decryption path; new exports use only phrases. The decrypted payload and room/pairing import policy remain version 1. New phrase transfers require both phones to be updated to 0.3.11.

TransferScreen runs encryption and unlocking in detached tasks with cancellation checks and original request UUIDs. Closed/backgrounded or superseded screens discard results before file writes or review publication. The non-interruptible PBKDF2 call may finish after cancellation, but its result is discarded. Only an encrypted URL enters the share sheet; the phrase stays in temporary sensitive view state with explicit local-only clipboard copying and five-minute clipboard expiry. A retained file and phrase have no global expiry or single-use enforcement. See [source and security audit](atv-remote-transfer-phrase-audit.md).

## Compatibility and remaining validation

Keep **S95BD** as the user's friendly display name. The Samsung adapter does not currently fetch the full hardware model or firmware; record these from each TV's own settings during testing. **S60** is ambiguous and has no confirmed compatibility status. A Samsung is a candidate only if it supports the secure Tizen remote interface this adapter uses.

The native code implements real connections and commands. The user reported Samsung volume/navigation working and Samsung off plus Apple TV Apps failing on the previous build. The 0.3 changes address verified protocol gaps; they have not yet been checked on those physical TVs by this workspace. Required checks include independent multi-TV routing, wrong-PIN and TV-denial recovery, reconnect after sleep/reboot, certificate-change rejection, app background/foreground, Local Network denial/recovery, room persistence, keyboard Unicode, installed apps, and observed power/input behavior. Record model, firmware, iOS version, and exact failure messages for each device.

Home uses native UIKit single/double/hold recognizers with failure dependencies so a double tap or hold does not also send a single Home press. Each pending touch captures navigation type, device identity and connection epoch; changing any cancels recognition. The store validates that capture again before dispatch. VoiceOver supports Home, App switcher and Control Center actions. These captures and restart targets are transient and do not change the shared registry or pairing payload.

App backgrounding now cancels commands, restart workflows, discovery and interactive connection setup while retaining tool/transfer presentations, local drafts and established outbound TV connections and their callback epochs. Automatic activation connects only disconnected/failed selected devices permitted by manual intent; established and pending sessions are not replaced. Peer closure callbacks remain authoritative. Already-started Apple button sequences retain their original driver/session and can finish key-up; canceled UI request IDs discard their results. Restart workflows remain directly cancellable and stop between checked steps. Uncertain Samsung writes retain their existing conservative cancellation/connection-reset behavior. No background mode, endless execution, heartbeat loop or vendor change is added. iOS suspension/termination can still end a socket; cold launch reconnects with existing Keychain pairings.

As of 0.3.39, Auto is gated off by AutoRoomPolicy.isAvailable. The room Picker omits its tag, startup clears its old local preference while retaining the saved room, and programmatic Auto selection is rejected. The following Auto implementation remains dormant for later work. Auto preference was a separate local UserDefaults boolean; room registry and transfer payload schemas remain unchanged. The main label uses a single line **Auto · [room]**, **Auto · Searching…** or **Auto · Offline**; settings keeps the actual room name. Automatic selection uses deduplicated saved room slots, excludes manual disconnects, authenticates with existing credentials only, and bounds trials to four. Failed trials free a slot; first success cancels further scheduling and disposes successful late losers. Registry/run/active checks precede handoff. The winner's observer has a unique instance token gated until an atomic main-store ownership/room-pair commit, preventing discarded trial callbacks from owning a manual connection with the same device ID or epoch. Only the old driver instance is closed; the winner is reused. Current healthy rooms stay stable. Fifteen-second backoff and modal/command/background guards limit rescans.

Foreground status polling reads each selected driver's established-session snapshot every two seconds with request/device/epoch checks. Driver events remain immediate; pending/error messages retain callback ownership and polls cannot invent successful authentication. No extra app-list, power, ping or HID request is sent per tick. Polls stop in the background. Silent peer/network failures still depend on protocol/OS reporting and existing timeout handling. Samsung trials hold returned tokens privately; only a selected winner may refresh a still-existing token/certificate pair through a non-suspending Keychain actor check/update. Changed/deleted records reject refresh, and losers never persist a token. The frozen vendor remains unchanged.

## Room editor selectors

The room editor uses the same explicit menu layout for Samsung TV and Apple TV: type label, selected device name, separate smaller IP address and a trailing chevron. Both menus fill the available width and keep their text and chevrons aligned regardless of name length. Selections edit the local room draft until Save; Cancel discards them. None remains available for either slot.

## Connection status after off attempts

Before an Apple TV sleep/off or Samsung power-toggle send, the store schedules one read of the captured driver session after a one-second delay. Power delivery remains one-shot, including an uncertain/error result. The observer uses only local transport state, with no ping, HID, wake, discovery, reconnect or automatic resend. The result can downgrade a connected dot but cannot manufacture a handshake or overwrite a pending/error state. A live standby connection stays green. Selection epoch, driver callback instance, device ID, lifecycle request and per-kind check request guard both sides of the awaited read. A new off attempt supersedes only that kind's earlier check; selection/reconnection/manual disconnect/background cancels it. The regular two-second monitor and immediate driver callbacks remain. A one-second observation does not guarantee silent peer-loss detection within one second.

## Temporary wake confirmation

A successful Samsung wake submission displays a noninteractive bottom overlay in Power for three seconds. It adds no List row or safe-area inset, so appearing and expiring do not move the controls. The icon/spinner share a fixed 22-point slot. A cancellable store timer has its own request identity; newer wake attempts and Samsung selection/setup/cancellation changes clear the previous confirmation, and an older expiry cannot clear a newer message. The shared feedback is cleared only while it still equals that confirmation. Error diagnostics remain persistent. This feedback does not assert that the television woke.

The complete decrypted payload is validated before any selection filter; room references and credentials are filtered to the chosen TVs and validated again. The version-1 payload fields and version-2 encrypted envelope are retained, and legacy encrypted files still decode. Export authentication, phrase generation, detached cryptography, protected files, explicit import approval and recovery remain.

## Saving Samsung wake settings
